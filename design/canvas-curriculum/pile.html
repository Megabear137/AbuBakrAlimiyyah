Four spines

Option 2 of the first round with four kinds of book. Each spine comes with a cover drawn to match, so the book drawn from the pile is the one you clicked. Round 1

A · Simple B · Manuscript C · Madrasah print D · Subject jackets
A

Simple

Flat cloth in six quiet colours, a lighter strip at the end, the title in the page's sans and the subject in small capitals. No tooling at all, and the cover is just as plain: the subject at the top and the title set low. The easiest to read, and the most modern.

B

Manuscript

Unbound quires of parchment, each tucked into a leather flap and tied with a red cord. Their leaves show as fine lines, the title is written in italic ink and the subject in a red rubric, and the pile leans more than the others. The cover is a ruled leaf in red and black ink. The most handmade and traditional.

Curriculum

C

Madrasah print

The cloth bindings of the printed texts a student actually carries: green, maroon, navy, with a visible weave, triple gold bands at each end and the title in capitals inside a gilt label. The cover repeats the bands and the label. Recognisable to anyone who has studied from these books.

D

Subject jackets

Cream jackets with a colour block per subject and the year's numeral at the end, with a key under the tabs, so the pile reads as a timetable: two Arabic books this year, one Nahw. The subject keeps its colour in every year. The most informative at a glance.
