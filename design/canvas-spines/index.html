Curriculum · the spines

Green and gold

Four Islamic bindings in dark green goatskin and gold, each shown on the shelf as it stands and then close up. The titles are larger than today's and set in solid colour, not a gilt gradient, so they read at a glance.

1 · Tooled goatskin 2 · Paper label 3 · Gilt bands 4 · Mihrab
1

Tooled goatskin

The closest to a Mamluk binding and to today's spine. A gilt frame with a braided border running down both edges, a gold pendant at the head and a small lozenge at the foot, and the title in a dark green ogee cartouche with a double gold line. The title is pale gold on near-black green, about 12:1. Hover a book on the shelf to lean it out.

2

Paper label

Ottoman in feeling. An Ottoman tulip in gold at the head, a double gilt frame with dots at its corners, and the title printed in dark green on a cream paper label with lobed ends, the way many madrasa kitabs are labelled. Dark ink on cream is the easiest of the four to read, about 12:1, and the label brightens the shelf.

3

Gilt bands

A modern Arabic multi-volume set: green cloth with solid gold panels at the head and foot. The head panel has a green pointed-arch window with a gold leaf in it, and the foot panel carries the subject in dark green on gold. The title is in gold capitals (Gilda Display, the section-title face) between two gold rules, about 9:1. The boldest of the four, and the shelf reads as a matching set.

4

Mihrab

The spine is one tall pointed arch, like the testimony niches further down the page, with a lamp hanging from its apex and a stepped base. There is a small arcade at the head, a border of punched gold dots, and the subject in a gold capsule at the foot. The title is cream inside the arch, about 8:1.
