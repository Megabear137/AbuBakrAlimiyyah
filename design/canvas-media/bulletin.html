Design round 3 · Articles

Articles on a bulletin board

Five ways to pin the Articles section to a board. Each keeps what the live section has: the newest article as a large feature, the rest in a carousel with its arrows in a small head above, and the Brothers / Sisters switch. The carousels and switches all work. Titles, dates and authors are sample placeholders, not real content.

A · Cork in an oak frame B · Green felt & gold ribbons C · Masjid noticeboard D · Pegboard, tape & clip E · Clippings on cork
A

Cork in an oak frame

The classic board. Cork sits in a frame of the same dark wood as the curriculum shelf. The feature is a cream letter held by two brass pushpins under a green-and-gold rule, with a gold rosette reading “New”. The other articles are index cards in white, mint and pale gold, each on one pin and slightly askew. Hovering a card straightens it. The most familiar option, and the closest match to the shelf.

B

Green felt & gold ribbons

A memo board: deep green felt in a gilt frame, crossed by gold ribbons with brass studs where they meet. Nothing is pinned through. Sheets are tucked under ribbons at their corners. The feature has the double gilt frame of the kitab spines, with the byline and button set apart on the right. The most formal option. It is in the site's own green and gold.

C

Masjid noticeboard

The locked, glass-fronted case in a masjid foyer. It has a wooden cabinet, brass hinges and a lock, and a brass plaque that changes with the switch. Inside, notices are fixed flat to sage linen with brass drawing pins: four on the poster-style feature and two on each smaller notice. A sheen of glass lies over everything but lets clicks through. Neat and upright, with barely any tilt. It will feel familiar to anyone who attends the masjid.

D

Pegboard, tape & clip

A light studio board: a cream pegboard in a birch frame. The feature is today's green card, unchanged, hung from a brass bulldog clip with a cream sheet behind it. The other articles are held up with gold and mint washi tape. The lightest and most modern option. It changes the live section the least.

Articles

E

Clippings on cork

Articles cut from a newspaper and pinned up with map pins. The paper has torn edges. The feature is a front-page clipping with a masthead, a large headline and a drop capital, with the byline in a side column. The other clippings are smaller, each on a green, gold or red pin. The cork is lighter and has no frame. The most playful option. The newspaper look suits articles well.
