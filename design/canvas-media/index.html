Five ways to present Articles & Videos

Each option below is built on the live stylesheet and swaps between a Brothers and a Sisters library. Every switch works — try it. Titles, durations and authors are sample placeholders, not real content.

1 · Segmented pill 2 · Reading room 3 · Carousel rows 4 · Two arches 5 · Library rail
1

Segmented pill

One centred Brothers / Sisters switch above both sections — a green knob slides between them and both sections change together. Articles lead with a featured green card (like Details); videos sit in a three-up grid of white cards. The quietest option: closest to the page as it stands.

Reading room

Articles and videos share one green panel with an inner gilt line. The switch is a tab pill straddling the panel's top edge, with the Arabic beside each label. Articles read as a numbered contents list; videos as a featured lesson with three more beneath. Compact: one section instead of two.

    3

    Carousel rows

    Built like Teachers and Alumni: a heading on the left, controls on the right. Each section's head carries a small Brothers ○ Sisters toggle next to the carousel arrows — the two toggles stay in sync. Articles use the testimony card's fading gold edge; videos are wide cards with the caption over the picture. Most consistent with the carousels already on the page.

    4

    Two arches

    The switch is the centrepiece: two mihrab arches, one for each library, with a count of what's inside. The chosen arch turns green. Articles are cream folios in a double gilt frame with the subject in a pointed cartouche — the kitab language of the bookshelf. Videos: a large player with an "Up next" list. The most ornamental; makes the two libraries feel like two rooms.

    5

    Library rail

    Articles and videos become one searchable library. A sticky rail on the left holds the Brothers / Sisters choice (with counts) and a filter for All, Articles or Videos; the feed on the right mixes both, each marked with its type. Scales best once there are dozens of posts — could become its own page later.
